From medical knowledge to a claim

Diagnosis is not the same as service, setting, or payment.

There is no single “Medicaid charge-code” taxonomy.

Medicaid claims combine national code sets with state-specific coverage, fee schedules, provider manuals, authorization requirements, and claim edits. A provider’s dollar charge is another field—not the code itself.

01
What condition is being described?
ICD-11 · ICD-10-CM

Diagnosis and health condition

ICD classifies diseases, injuries, symptoms, and related health information. ICD-11 is the global WHO standard; ICD-10-CM remains the U.S. clinical modification used for diagnosis coding.

02
What inpatient procedure occurred?
ICD-10-PCS

Hospital inpatient procedures

ICD-10-PCS is a separate U.S. procedure classification for inpatient hospital reporting. It is not the code system generally used for office visits, outpatient imaging, supplies, or professional services.

03
What service, supply, or item was furnished?
HCPCS Level I & II

Professional services and billable items

HCPCS Level I is CPT, maintained by the AMA for professional services and procedures. CMS maintains alphanumeric HCPCS Level II for many supplies, products, equipment, ambulance services, and other items.

04
Where, how, and with what product?
NDC · POS · revenue codes · modifiers

Claim context

Additional fields identify the drug product, care setting, facility department, professional or technical component, units, and other details needed to interpret a claim correctly.

05
Is it covered, payable, and correctly combined?
Maryland Medicaid · NCCI

State payment and claim-edit layer

Maryland applies program-specific fee schedules, manuals, clinical criteria, prior authorization, and provider rules. Medicaid NCCI adds procedure-to-procedure and medically unlikely edits to applicable fee-for-service HCPCS/CPT claims.
